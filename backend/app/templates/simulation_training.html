1. Inspect the sender

Attackers copy familiar display names and branding. Check the full sender address before trusting the message.

2. Inspect the link destination

Inspect the real destination before opening a link. When possible, use a trusted bookmark or the official application instead.

3. Pause when a message creates urgency

Threats of account closure, unexpected invoices, prizes, and immediate deadlines are common pressure tactics. A request becoming urgent does not make it trustworthy.

4. Verify through a known channel

Open the official website or application directly using a saved bookmark. Contact the supposed sender through a trusted directory—not through details in the suspicious message.

5. Report suspicious messages

Report the message using your organization’s approved process. Early reporting helps protect other recipients and gives the security team useful context.
